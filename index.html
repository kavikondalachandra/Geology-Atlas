    Earth's Material Triad: Rocks, Minerals & Soils

    Earth's solid surface is scientifically organized into three fundamental, interconnected domains. Explore the petrographic formation pathways of rocks, the 6,200+ IMA-recognized mineral species, and the 12 global USDA soil orders with an interactive soil texture calculator.

    Petrology & Crustal Evolution

    All Rocks on Earth Derive From Three Fundamental Genetic Processes

    While geologists recognize hundreds of specific rock varieties and micro-textures, every rock on our planet originates from either the cooling of molten silicate melts (Igneous), the mechanical and biogenic consolidation of surface debris (Sedimentary), or the thermal-baric recrystallization of pre-existing protoliths (Metamorphic).

    Magmatic Genesis

    Igneous Rocks: Molten Solidification

    Formed when high-temperature silicate magma cools and crystallizes. Divided by cooling depth and cooling velocity into two realms:

    Intrusive / Plutonic: Cools slowly deep within magma chambers over thousands of years, growing interlocking macroscopic crystals (Phaneritic texture).
    Extrusive / Volcanic: Erupts onto the surface or ocean floor, quenching rapidly into microscopic crystals (Aphanitic) or amorphous volcanic glass.
    Bowen's Reaction Series: Governs mineral crystallization temperature from high-T olivine & Ca-plagioclase down to low-T quartz & K-feldspar.
    Surface Deposition

    Sedimentary Rocks: Lithified Strata

    Formed at ambient surface conditions through the weathering, fluvial/eolian transport, burial, compaction, and cementation of surface particles:

    Clastic / Detrital (Wentworth Scale): Classified mechanically by particle diameter: Clay (<0.004 mm) ➔ Silt ➔ Sand (0.063–2 mm) ➔ Gravel (>2 mm).
    Biochemical & Biogenic: Accumulation of calcite shells, planktonic micro-fossils (coccolithophores), and swamp vegetation under anaerobic burial.
    Chemical Precipitates: Direct crystalline evaporation from hypersaline seas and hydrothermal hot springs.
    Thermal-Baric Transformation

    Metamorphic Rocks: Solid-State Alteration

    Formed when pre-existing igneous, sedimentary, or metamorphic protoliths are subjected to differential stress, temperatures of 200°C–800°C, and reactive fluids without melting:

    Foliated (Differential Tectonic Stress): Directed pressure forces platy micas and clays to align into parallel planar bands, progressing with metamorphic grade:
    Non-Foliated (Equidimensional Recrystallization): Forms under uniform confining pressure or contact thermal baking around intrusive plutons:
    The Rock Cycle: Any rock type can be transformed into any other through tectonic uplift, subduction, weathering, or melting.
    IMA Mineralogical Taxonomy

    Over 6,200 Approved Mineral Species: The Chemical Architecture of Earth

    The International Mineralogical Association (IMA) recognizes over 6,200 valid mineral species—each with a defined chemical stoichiometry and internal crystallographic unit cell. Minerals are scientifically categorized into eight primary chemical classes based on their dominant anion, with silicates constituting over 90% of Earth's crust.

    01

    Silicates [SiO₄]⁴⁻

    >90% of Earth's Crust

    Silicon-oxygen tetrahedra linked into 3D frameworks (Tectosilicates), 2D sheets (Phyllosilicates), single/double chains (Inosilicates), rings (Cyclosilicates), or isolated units (Nesosilicates).

    02

    Oxides & Hydroxides

    Primary Metallic Ores

    Oxygen anions bonded directly to metal cations. Form dense, hard, durable ore minerals and gemstones.

    03

    Sulfides & Sulfosalts

    Hydrothermal Vein Ores

    Sulfur (S²⁻) bonded to transition metals. Possess high specific gravity, metallic luster, and colored streaks.

    04

    Carbonates [CO₃]²⁻

    Marine & Secondary Zones

    Carbon-oxygen planar radicals bonded to divalent cations. Effervesce readily in dilute acid.

    05

    Halides (F⁻, Cl⁻)

    Evaporite Marine Salts

    Electronegative halogens bonded to alkali metals. Exhibit cubic or octahedral cleavage and water solubility.

    06

    Sulfates [SO₄]²⁻

    Basin Evaporites & Spar

    Tetrahedral sulfate groups bonded to alkaline earths. Soft or high-density industrial minerals.

    07

    Phosphates [PO₄]³⁻

    Biological & Igneous

    Phosphorus-oxygen tetrahedra. Crucial agricultural fertilizer source and primary constituent of vertebrate bones.

    08

    Native Elements

    Pure Uncombined Matter

    Crystals formed from a single pure elemental substance. Possess extreme density, electrical conductivity, or hardness.

    Pedology & Soil Taxonomy

    The 12 USDA Global Soil Orders & The Textural Triangle

    Under the international USDA Soil Taxonomy, the world's soils are classified into 12 major orders based on diagnostic horizons, weathering intensity, and environmental regimes. At a localized agricultural scale, physical behavior is dictated by the proportion of sand, silt, and clay across the 12 USDA Textural Classes.

    The 12 Global USDA Soil Orders

    01
    Alfisols
    Moderate Leaching · High Base Saturation

    Fertile soils formed under temperate deciduous forests. Feature an illuvial clay subsoil (argillic horizon) with high native nutrient fertility.

    Diagnostic: Ochric epipedon, Argillic horizon
    02
    Andisols
    Volcanic Ash · Allophane Rich

    Formed in volcanic ash tephra ejecta. Rich in amorphous allophane and imogolite minerals with exceptionally high water storage and phosphorus fixation.

    Diagnostic: Andic properties, Melanic epipedon
    03
    Aridisols
    Arid Climates · Calcic & Saline

    Desert soils of hyper-arid to semi-arid regions. Limited leaching leads to accumulation of subsurface carbonates, gypsum, or soluble salts.

    Diagnostic: Ochric epipedon, Calcic/Gypsic/Salic
    04
    Entisols
    Recent Embryonic · Minimal Horizons

    Young mineral soils showing little or no genetic horizon development. Found on dynamic river sandbars, active sand dunes, and steep eroding slopes.

    Diagnostic: Ochric epipedon, No diagnostic B horizon
    05
    Gelisols
    Permafrost Soils · Cryoturbation

    Tundra soils with perennially frozen ground within 100 cm of the surface. Characterized by frost churning, ice wedges, and large organic carbon pools.

    Diagnostic: Permafrost table, Cryoturbation features
    06
    Histosols
    Organic Soils · Wetlands & Bogs

    Soils containing >20–30% organic carbon by weight. Formed in water-logged anaerobic fens and bogs; critical global terrestrial carbon sinks.

    Diagnostic: Histic epipedon (>40 cm organic matter)
    07
    Inceptisols
    Beginning Horizons · Young Surfaces

    Embryonic soils showing the inception of horizon differentiation (such as a cambic B horizon) without advanced illuvial clay or spodic accumulation.

    Diagnostic: Cambic B horizon, Ochric epipedon
    08
    Mollisols
    Prairie Grasslands · Prime Breadbaskets

    The world's premier agricultural soils (Chernozems). Feature a deep, dark, humus-rich mollic epipedon with >50% base saturation from grass root decay.

    Diagnostic: Mollic epipedon (Thick, dark, soft, fertile)
    09
    Oxisols
    Extreme Weathering · Tropical Laterites

    The most intensely leached and chemically weathered soils on Earth. Dominated by low-activity kaolinite and red/yellow iron-aluminum oxides.

    Diagnostic: Oxic horizon, Low CEC, Quartz residues
    10
    Spodosols
    Boreal Coniferous · Ash-Grey Podzols

    Acidic soils of cold, humid pine forests. Organic acids leach iron and aluminum from a bleached ash-grey E horizon into an illuvial spodic subsoil.

    Diagnostic: Albic E horizon over Spodic Bh/Bs horizon
    11
    Ultisols
    Warm Subtropics · Acidic Red Clays

    Intensely weathered forest soils of warm temperate and subtropical zones. Possess an argillic clay horizon with <35% base saturation.

    Diagnostic: Argillic horizon with low base saturation
    12
    Vertisols
    Shrink-Swell Clay · Black Cotton Soil

    Clay-rich soils (>30% clay) dominated by smectite. Form deep dry-season desiccation cracks and slickenside shear planes as they turn over.

    Diagnostic: Slickensides, Wedge structure, Gilgai
